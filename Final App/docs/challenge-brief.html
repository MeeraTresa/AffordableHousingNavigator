Affordable Housing Stability

Affordable Housing Stability & Eviction Prevention Navigator

More than half of low-income renters in America spend over 50% of their paycheck on rent — leaving families with one missed work shift or unexpected emergency away from an eviction filing. Across major cities, households appearing in housing court for nonpayment frequently live in properties with documented municipal code violations such as no heat, raw sewage backups, toxic mold or unsafe wiring.

Under state and municipal housing codes, those hazards unlock emergency rent relief and legal defenses. However, code-enforcement records and court dockets typically operate in separate administrative silos. Bridging these data streams before a court hearing is critical in delivering relief and protection that the law intended.

The challenge

Legal-aid clinics and city housing navigators know that aid exists, but they struggle to obtain corroborated proof of property conditions and code violations, real-time matches to solve stabilization funds, and on-demand guidance when case details change under tight legal deadlines. With waiting rooms full of families and scarce relief funds, clinics have only minutes to isolate active residential safety hazards from irrelevant or closed complaint data.

Your mission

Build a stabilization navigator for a legal-aid paralegal or municipal housing counselor — not a dashboard, and not a single query with a chat box on it. An agent that an intake worker can hold a working conversation with, that reaches for real complaint narratives, housing statutes, and semantic search to answer.

Requirements & deliverable

Required differentiator

BigQuery Vector Search. Tenants and inspectors describe the same hazard twenty different ways — your agent embeds and searches complaint narratives on meaning (AI.EMBED + VECTOR_SEARCH) rather than fragile keyword rules, then filters out closed, commercial, and "no violation found" decoys.

You in hand

A Tenant Stabilization Brief — corroborated 100-block residential complaint records, the matching habitability statute and emergency relief fund, why this household qualifies, who lives in the corridor (via a post-output equity audit), what the public record cannot prove alone, and a drafted legal-aid intake memo.
